# Dilemmas in a General Theory of Planning (1973)

"Professionals job was once seen as solving an assortment of problems that appeared to be definable, understandable and consensual. He was hired to eliminate those conditions that predominant opinion judged undesirable."

Now that these relatively easy problems have been dealt with, we have been turning our attention to others that are much more stubborn. There seems to be a growing realization that a weak strut in the professional's support system lies at the juncture where goal-formulation, problem-definition and equity issues meet. 

Goal Formulation: Must look at our objectives as critically as we look at our models and other inputs. Ask What do the systems do? rather than What are they made of? And then What should these systems do? 

Problem Definition: During industrial age, idea of planning was dominated by the pervasive idea of efficiency. Was fairly easy to get consensus on nature of problems in this period. Task could be assigned to the technically skilled. Recent years have been learning to ask whether what we are doing is the right thing to do. Have been learning to ask questions about the outputs of actions (not the reduction of resource inputs to a problem). Have learned that outputs of one systems may be inputs of another systems and learned to expand the boundaries of the systems we deal with.
