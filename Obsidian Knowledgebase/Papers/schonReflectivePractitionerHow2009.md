# The reflective practitioner: how professionals think in action (1983)

#### Reflection-in-action [p. 49 and following]

Spontaneous, intuitive performance of actions in everyday life. Cannot say what it is that we know. When trying to describe it, found at a loss. The **knowing is tacit** - implicitly in our action and our feel for the stuff with which we are dealing. 

_Reflection-in-action_: People thinking about what they are doing, sometimes even while doing it. Stimulated by surprise, they turn thought back on action. May ask themselves, for example, "What features do I notice when I recognize this thing?". As person is trying to make sense of it, he also reflects on the understandings which he surfaces. 

Phrase **tacit knowing**, invented by Michael Polanyi: Draws examples from ability to recognize a face among millions while not being able to tell how. Other example: Learn to use a tool: Feelings of which we are initially aware become internalized in our _tacit knowing_. 

In _design_ from Chris Alexander: We can often recognize and correct the "bad fit" of a form to its context, but we usually cannot describe by  what rules we find a fit bad or recognize the corrected form to be good. Geoffrey Vickers points out: "We can recognize and describe deviations from a norm very much more clearly than we can describe the norm itself". 

Knowing in these examples has the following properties: 
- There are actions, recognitions and judgments which we know how to carry out spontaneously
- We are often unaware of having learned to do these things
- In some cases we were once aware of the understandings which were subsequently internalized in our _feeling for the stuff of action_
This examples fall under **knowing-in-action**

**Reflection-in-action**
Sometimes we think about what we are doing, sometimes while doing it. During performance athletes for example reflect-on-action, sometimes reflect-in-action. 

Much reflection-in-action hinges on surprise. When intuitive performance leads to surprises, we may respond by reflection-in-action. For example: The designer, on the misfit they unintentionally created. 

Professionals might as well reflect-in-action. May not be rapid. It is bound by the "action-present" - the time in which action can still make a difference to the situation. May stretch over minutes, hours, days or weeks or months. The objects of his reflection are as varied. May reflect on tacit norms and appreciations which underlie a judgment, or on the strategies and theories implicit in a pattern of behavior. He may reflect on the feeling for a situation which has led him to adopt a particular course of action, or the way in which he has framed the problem he is trying to solve. 

Reflection-in-action in these several modes, is central to the art through which practitioners cope with the divergent situations of practice. 

When he finds himself stuck in a problematic situation which he cannot readily convert to a manageable problem, he may construct a new way of setting the problem - a new frame, which the author calls a _"frame experiment"_ - he tries to impose on the situation. 

When confronted with demands that seem incompatible or inconsistent, he may respond by reflection on the appreciations which he and others have brought to the situation. 

Sometimes the practitioner carries out an experiment on the spot to test a new hypothesis and generate a new understanding of the phenomena and a change in the situation. 

When someone reflects-in-action, he becomes a researcher in the practice context. He does not keep means and ends separate, but defines them interactively as he frames a problematic situation. He does not separate thinking from doing. Because his experimenting is a kind of action, implementation is build into his inquiry. Thus reflection-in-action can proceed, even in situations of uncertainty or uniqueness. 

Although it is an extraordinary process, it is not a rare event, for some practitioners even the core of practice.
