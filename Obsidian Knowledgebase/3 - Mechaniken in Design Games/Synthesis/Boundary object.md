[[brandtFacilitatingCollaborationDesign2004a]]
Objects acting as [[Tangible Game Material]]. They allow players to find out how other interpret the material. Those objects carry shared meaning. They make arguments tangible and help participants to focus. They create a common ground everybody can relate to and act as [[things-to-think-with]] They allow different participants to read and interpret the material differently. They are rich enough in content to span the gap between different understandings. 

[[brandtDesigningExploratoryDesign2006]]
Game state and [[Game pieces]] are placed centrally so every participant can see the current state of information. [[Game pieces]] are [[abstracted]] so participants can choose what they represent ([[Open to interpretation]]). 

[[Game pieces]] function as boundary objects, shared objects that allow different interpretations at the same time.

[[kessnerWickedPlayWicked2020]]
[[Broken games]] are incomplete games - missing or ill-aligned mechanics, lack of clear rules, etc. - which **participants must "fix"** as part of a larger task [p.3]. Broken games function as [[things-to-think-with]] - players take the [[Roles]] of player-designers who must redesign the broken game to align to certain learning goals [p.3]. This shift in task-participant structure creates a playful game space in which design novices are immersed in designerly thought and activity. [p.3] [[Informal atmosphere]]
