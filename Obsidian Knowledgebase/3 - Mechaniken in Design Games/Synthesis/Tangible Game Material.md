[[brandtFacilitatingCollaborationDesign2004a]]
Like [[Game board]], [[cards]]. They abstract real-life objects but are not too concrete to be open to interpretation. E.g. Game boards with concentric circles when designing an office layout. Can be interpreted as space centering around the individual worker vs. multiple circles can be interpreted as groups working together. 

Being tangible helps all participants to keep track of their functions and role within the [[negotiation]]. 

They also function as [[Boundary object]] between people with various competencies. They allow players to also find out how other interpret the material. Those objects carry shared meaning. They make arguments tangible and help participants to focus. They create a common ground everybody can relate to and act as [[things-to-think-with]] They allow different participants to read and interpret the material differently. They are rich enough in content to span the gap between different understandings.

Concept Design Games create configurations using [[Game pieces]] on a [[Game board]]. Pieces and board can be [[abstracted]] to eliminate functional knowledge and former experiences players bring with them. 

[[User Game]] has Moment [[cards]] and Sign-[[cards]] to provide [[Input]] for a conceptual framework for stories created by the participants. Game state and [[Game pieces]] are placed centrally so every participant can see the current state of information. 

[[Game pieces]] are [[abstracted]] so participants can choose what they represent ([[Open to interpretation]], [[Boundary object]]). [[Game board]] are interpreted by the [[Players]]. 

Videos and images from field work are powerful [[Game pieces]]. Using tangible pieces allow participants to use visual, auditory and tactile senses, which evoke more reflections and comments than otherwise. 

Game materials help bridge the gap between different languages of the participants.

[[royCardbasedDesignTools2019]]
**Externalized representations** are important during design development. One approach is to organize blocks of content into **tangible [[cards]]**. Simply **shuffling through the cards** and posing the questions creates an opportunity to remember to address more issues than might be in the initial project brief. [p.6].

[[Cards]] can be sorted and handled in multi dimensional physical ways and thus considered by human minds accordingly. Also allows to easily violate categories of information, which allows the brains creativity to come in and repair them in an ad hoc manner [p.6]. (Rothstein 2012)

[[Cards]] thus facilitate creative combinations of information and ideas. They provide a **common basis for understanding and communication** in a team. They are semi-structured between blank post-its and detailed instruction manuals. 
However they can also overload participants with too much information, may oversimplify some information and are difficult to change and update. [p.7]
