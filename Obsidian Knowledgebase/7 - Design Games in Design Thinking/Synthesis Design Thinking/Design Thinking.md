[[Brown2008]]
Describes Design thinking as _"a discipline that uses the designer's sensibility and methods to match people's needs with what is technologically feasible and what a viable business strategy can convert into customer value and market opporunity"_. [p.3]

Mentions [[brands]] that create breakthrough ideas by deeply understand customers and using design to innovate and build value [p.7]

Mentions [[Wicked problems]] and that they have people at their heart. [[Design Thinking]] is an approach to find a human-centered, creative, iterative and practical idea for solutions [p.10]

[[brownDesignThinkingSocial2010]]
"_Design thinking_ (...) adresses the needs of the people who will consume a product or service and the infrastructure that enables it".

[[kimbellRethinkingDesignThinking2011]]
It is a human centered, iterative approach that focuses on understanding user needs, generating ideas and testing prototypes. It fosters multidisciplinary collaboration and questions the status quo by asking "what if?" questions. [p.4]

_design thinking_ is most often used to focus on challenges businesses face and discussed within a managerialist framework [p.10]. Works do not draw on a research base, but despite that, gained legitimacy among a wide base of designers and organizations [p.11]

Brown presented his findings as answer for organizations wanting to innovate and societies concerned with complex issues (**Wicked problems?**) [p.11] While no academic sources are claimed, still concepts mirrors many findings from academia [p.11]. Particular emphasis is placed on **seeing _design thinking_ as a human-centered activity**.

**A successful outcome exist at the intersection of desirable from users, technically feasible and viable for the business** [p.11]

Different ways of describing it are: [p.14] 
- **Design thinking as a cognitive style**: Focus on problem solving. Key concepts: Design ability as a form of intelligence, abductive thinking. (Schön, Cross, Dorst)
- **Design thinking as a general theory of design**: Design as a field or discipline. Focus on taming wicked problems. (Buchanan) (-> Designerly Thinking)
- **Design thinking as an organizational resource**: Focuses on businesses in need of innovation. Key concepts are: **Visualization, prototyping, empathy, integrative thinking, abductive thinking**. (**Brown**, Martin)

[[johansson-skoldbergDesignThinkingPresent2013a]]
_Design thinking_ as a management discourse appeared in the early 2000's. 
[[IDEO]], using its practical experience and partnership with the Stanford University, re-branded itself as "an innovation company", introducing _design thinking_ into the academic innovation discourse as an alternative to strategic management models gaining traction in 2009 [p.7]. Design thinking grew in popularity as an innovation tool by making design methodologies accessible to non-designers and managers rather than limiting them to creative professionals [p.8]. This concept evolved through three different origins: IDEO’s published set of methodologies, work culture and infrastructure - labelled as _design thinking_ by the CEO Tim Brown [p.8]. **Roger Martins description of _design thinking_ as a cycle of generating ideas (abduction), predicting consequences (deduction), and testing and generalizing (induction) [p.8]. **

[[micheliDoingDesignThinking2019]]
The term has varied origins, in academic writing many write about _designerly thinking_ while _design thinking_ established itself recently in management and is described as "an approach to innovation and creative problem-solving founded in designers' processes and practices" [p.2]

Three definitions of _design thinking_ have been cited the most in literature [p.8]: 
- (1) Brown 2008: design thinking is "a discipline that uses the designers' sensibility and methods to match people's needs with what is technologically feasible and what a viable business strategy can convert into customer value and market opportunity"
- (2) Lockwood 2010: design thinking is "a human-centered innovation process that emphasizes observation, collaboration, fast learning, visualization of ideas, rapid concept prototyping, and concurrent business analysis"
- (3) Martin 2009: defines design thinking as "the productive mix of analytical thinking and intuitive thinking"
