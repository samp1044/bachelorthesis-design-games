[[johansson-skoldbergDesignThinkingPresent2013a]]
Proposes to discuss design thinking as two distinct discourses: 
- _Designerly thinking_ is used to describe the research rooted in academia about the designers practice and theoretic discussions about it. 
- _Design thinking_ for discourse beyond the design practice that is mostly by people without academic background, particularly in management. [p.3]

_Designerly thinking_ is rooted in over 50 years of academic research, with first resources mentioned in 1969 and a purely academic aim. Until Buchanan in 1992 a step-by-step approach with two distinct phases of problem definition, followed by problem solution, was the accepted model. Buchanan build on the older _wicked problem approach_ and suggested "placements" to contextualize design situations, allowing problem definition and solution to happen simultaneously. Buchanan further identified the four distinct areas of design thinking as _symbolic and visual communications_, _material objects_, _activities and organizational services_, _complex systems or environments for living, working, playing and learning_. [p.5]. Lawson and Cross in 2005 and 2011 derived practical, observation-based patterns from real-world experience, with Lawson suggesting a model of the design process as a series of process-driven steps and Cross framing it as a recursive strategy [p.5]. Krippendorff in 2006 initiated a "semantic turn" by defining design as the creation of meaning rather than physical artifacts. [p.6]

_Design thinking_ as a management discourse appeared in the early 2000s. 
IDEO, using its practical experience and partnership with the Standford University, rebranded itself as "an innovation company", introducing _design thinking_ into the academic innovation discourse as an alternative to strategic management models gaining traction in 2009 [p.7]. Design thinking grew in popularity as an innovation tool by making design methodologies accessible to non-designers and managers rather than limiting them to creative professionals [p.8]. This concept evolved through three different origins: IDEO’s published set of methodologies, work culture and infrastructure - labelled as _design thinking_ by the CEO Tim Brown [p.8]. Roger Martins description of _design thinking_ as a cycle of generating ideas (abduction), predicting consequences (deduction), and testing and generalizing (induction) [p.8].

[[micheliDoingDesignThinking2019]]
Notes that there is no generally accepted definition for _design thinking_ [p.2]. Among the literature some see it as organizational attribute, some focus on the traits at an individual level, some concentrate on tooling and some see it as (design) culture [p.2]. The term has varied origins, in academic writing many write about _designerly thinking_ while _design thinking_ established itself recently in management and is described as "an approach to innovation and creative problem-solving founded in designers' processes and practicses" [p.2]. **Performed a literature review.**

Three definitions of _design thinking_ have been cited the most in literature [p.8]: 
- (1) Brown 2008: design thinking is "a discipline that uses the designers' sensibility and methods to match people's needs with what is technologically feasible and what a viable business strategy can convert into customer value and market opportunity"
- (2) Lockwood 2010: design thinking is "a human-centered innovation process that emphasizes observation, collaboration, fast learning, visualization of ideas, rapid concept prototyping, and concurrent business analysis"
- (3) Martin 2009: defines design thinking as "the productive mix of analytical thinking and intuitive thinking"

Applied models most frequently cited are: [p.8]
- **IDEO**: Inspiration, ideation, implementation
- **Stanford Design School**: Empathy, define, ideate, prototype, test
- **IBM**: Understand, explore, prototype, evaluate
The models share a common core structure and follow three main phases: [p.8]
- **Problem exploration**: Initial research to understand the problem to solve
- **Ideation**: Generate creative solutions and alternative ideas
- **Implementation and testing**: Build prototypes and continuously refine them through iteration

Through a systematic literature review and following card sorting exercise 10 attributes of _design thinking_ and 8 _tools_ have been identified [p.7] 
- **Creativity and Innovation**: Creativity and innovation is a central theme in _design thinking_ literature and practice, acting as both core drivers and outcomes achieved through methods like prototyping, trial-and-error, and abductive logic. 
- **User centeredness and involvement**: User centeredness is a core value of _design thinking_. Some frameworks advocate for direct involvement, referred to as participatory-, or co-design. Others describe empathy as "core value of human-centeredness". 
- **Problem solving**: _Design thinking_ is considered as an alternative to analytical, linear ways of solving problems, especially for addressing "wicked problems", where certain aspects can be improved but the problem not fully solved in a "right" way.
- **Iteration and experimentation**: _Design thinking_ utilizes an iterative, trial-and-error approach to solve problems. Tangible prototypes, mocks and sketches are used to experimentally explore ideas and get feedback from stakeholders. 
- **Interdisciplinary collaboration**: _Design thinking_ relies on bringing together people from different backgrounds into a cross-functional, multidisciplinary team to make sure the various perspectives are represented. 
- **Ability to visualize**: Visualizing concepts and ideas is central in _design thinking_ and can make abstract concepts feel real and vivid. This may be through physical representations, prototypes or sketches. 
- **Gestalt view**: _Design thinking_ incorporates an integrative approach that considers the context and systems in which a problem exists. It enables _design thinkers_ gain deeper understanding of a problems context and to challenge the problem statement, incorporating insights already gained into the reformulation of it.
- **Abductive reasoning**: Is assertion-based that imagines what might be rather than analyzing based on evidence of what is. Generates new knowledge by reframing existing assumptions to find best possible explanation or solution. 
- **Tolerance of ambiguity and failure**: Design thinkers must embrace ambiguity and failures as opportunity to learn early through iteration and low-cost trial-and-error cycles. [p.13]
- **Blending rationality and intuition**: Rather than replacing analytical logic, _design thinking_ dynamically blends rational, evaluative research with intuitive insights to drive innovation. 

Essential design thinking tools and methods found: [p.13]
- **Ethnographic methods**:  
	- _user centeredness and involvement_ Means to foster empathy 
	- _blend analysis and intuition_: Observational data complements quantitative data.
	- _gestalt view_: Understand problem in its context. 
	- _abductive reasoning_: Rich data can trigger "what if?" questions
- **Personas**:
	- _user centeredness and involvement_: Means to foster empathy
	- _interdisciplinary collaboration_: Serve as boundary-spanning object
	- _visualize_ key stakeholders
	- _gestalt view_: of the problem by ensuring users are represented in the process
- **Journey map**:
	- _user centeredness and involvement_: Foster empathy
	- _interdisciplinary collaboration_: Serve as a boundary-spanning object
	- _visualize_: Key stakeholders' experiences
	- _Iteration and experimentation_: by allowing to consider and test multiple user journeys
	- _gestalt view_: by encouraging contextual and temporal understanding of user experience
- **Brainstorming**: 
	- _abductive reasoning_: Provides a forum for that
	- _interdisciplinary collaboration_: Typically used during that
	- _blending of analysis and experimentation_: Reinforces through withholding judgement of individual ideas
- **Mind map**:
	- _interdisciplinary collaboration_: Serves as boundary-spanning object
	- _acceptance of ambiguity and failure_: by facilitating a process of sensemaking
	- _ability to visualize_ and structure complex systems and environments
	- _gestalt view_: by mapping multiple stakeholders' conceptualizations
- **Visualization**: 
	- _interdisciplinary collaboration_: Serves as boundary-spanning object
	- _ability to visualize_: underscores the importance to visualize solutions during ideation
	- _iteration and experimentation_: encouraged by informal nature of sketches
	- _user centeredness_, _gestalt view_, _blending of analysis and intuition_
- **Prototyping**:
	- _iteration and experimentation_ through provided physical means
	- _failure_ encouraged as early draft form
	- _interdisciplinary collaboration_ serves as boundary-spanning object
	- _ability to visualize_ underscores the importance to visualize
- **Experiments**: 
	- enable _iteration and experimentation_
	- _gestalt view_ through deep appreciation of the problem context
	- _failure_ encouraged for early draft form

Cluster analysis based on the 10 attributes. creativity and innvoation was present in all literature. user centeredness, problem solving, iteration and experimentation was present in 60% of artices. Those attributes have thus been excluded. Of the remaining 6 attributes, the following five clusters have been formed. These can be seen **to represent five perspectives on design thinking literature** [p.15]
- **Emphasis on interdisciplinary collaboration**: Cluster 1 highlights design thinking as an interdisciplinary team sport that breaks down organizational silos by training all employees to use design methods. Advocates argue that this scalable problem-solving methodology can be applied across any industry or context. However, critics note that widespread claims of its positive organizational impact remain largely speculative and lack empirical evidence. [p.16-17]
- **Emphasis on reclaiming design thinking as designers domain**: Cluster 2 advocates for reclaiming design thinking as a professionally trained skillset, arguing that core attributes like visualization cannot be separated from actual design practice. Authors in this group challenge the management-led idea that design thinking can easily be uncoupled from "design doing" or successfully adopted by non-designers without formal training. [p.17]
- **Emphasis on resilience in problem solving**: Cluster 3 highlights how design thinking serves as a cultural shift toward interdisciplinary collaboration and an increased tolerance for failure in complex environments, though debates continue regarding whether success requires professional design experience or simply a diverse mix of thinking styles. [p.18]
- **Emphasis on seeing and reflecting upon the whole**: Cluster 4 focuses on individual cognitive processes, emphasizing how professional design education leverages visualization and a holistic, gestalt view to navigate iterative cycles of action and reflection. [p.18]
- **Emphasis on learning to think like a designer**: Cluster 5 emphasizes a holistic, gestalt perspective that blends analysis and intuition, advocating that this mindset should be taught in business schools despite skepticism about whether non-designers can truly master the craft.[p.18]
