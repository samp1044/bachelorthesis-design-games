[Tim Brown - Design Thinking - 2008]
(Tim Brown is the CEO and president of IDEO)

Design thinking _"is a discipline that uses the designer's sensibility and methods to match people's needs with what is technologically feasible and what a viable business strategy can convert into customer value and market opporunity"_. [p.3]

Historically design has mainly played a role in making finished products aesthetically pleasing. In _design thinking_ design is part of the whole strategic process of coming up with new innovation. [p.3]

Example 1: Improving hand-over processes at a hospital. Nurses, doctors, administrators were part of the workshops together with designers from IDEO. Together with nurses problems in current shift changes have been identified [p.3]. Potential solutions were explored through brainstorming and rapid prototyping. Prototypes must be tangible (e.g. service changes were videotaped). Prototyping must not be expensive (e.g. ad-hoc taping together simple everyday objects) and only take so much effort to get feedback for further developing an idea or identifying new directions for the next prototypes. [p.4]

Example 2: Team began with a hunch on where it should focus. It set out to learn why customers behave as they did and spent time with all kinds of customers in doing so [p.5]. 
Application of design thinking not only led to a product but to a complete solution [p.7]. 

Design process is described as a "system of spaces" - differing from the typical linear and milestone based business process. Projects must pass through the three spaces: [p.5]
- **Inspiration**: Problem, opportunity that motivate the search for solutions
- **Ideation**: Generate, develop and test ideas
- **Implementation**: Charting a path to market

"Many of the world's most successful brands create breakthrough ideas that are inspired by a deep understanding of customers' lives and use the principles of design to innovate and build value" [p.7]

"Great design satisfies both our needs and our desires" [p.8]. "Design thinking is a tool for imagining these experiences as well as giving them a desirable form". [p.9]. 

(Wicked problems) have people at their heart. Design thinking is an approach to find a human-centered, creative, iterative and practical ideas for solutions. [p.10]

[[brownDesignThinkingSocial2010]] - Design Thinking for social innvoation - Tim Brown - 2010

"_Design thinking_ (...) adresses the needs of the people who will consume a product or service and the infrastructure that enables it".

**David Kelley, founder of Stanford University's Hasso Plattner Institute of Design (d.school)*

Design thinking is a process of overlapping spaces: 
- **Inspiration**: Discover peoples need. Through observation better insights are obtained than focus groups or surveys. 
- **Ideation**: Results of discovery are synthesized. **Divergent thinking** is important in this space. Better results are achieved by including diverse, multidisciplinary people. Brainstorming and visual representation of ideas are utilized. 
- **Implementation**: Ideas from ideation are iterated upon using prototyping which are tested and refined. After finishing the prototype a communication strategy is also conceived by the design team. 

The spaces are not followed in sequential but may be looped back towards and more than once. Usually **Inspiration** is the first phase in a new project however.

[[kimbellRethinkingDesignThinking2011]] - Kimbell - 2011
Within _design thinking_ "Design" should provide more than design. [p.3]
It is a human centered, iterative approach that focuses on understanding user needs, generating ideas and testing prototypes. It fosters multidisciplinary collaboration and questions the status quo by asking "what if?" questions. [p.4]

What _design thinking_ is supposed to be is, however, not well understood. Generalizing  _design thinking_ among the diversity of designers' practice is questionable and for all "user-centeredness" the designer is still emphasized as the main agent. [p.6] 

Research originates in the 1960s and focused on how designers design [p.6]. It gradually shifted into investigations to understand the practices by which designers went about designing [p.6]. Today _design thinking_ is most often used to focus on challenges businesses face and discussed within a managerialist framework [p.10]. Works do not draw on a research base, but despite that, gained legitimacy among a wide base of designers and organizations [p.11]. 
Brown presented his findings as answer for organizations wanting to innovate and societies concerned with complex issues [p.11] While no academic sources are claimed, still concepts mirrors many findings from academia [p.11]. Particular emphasis is placed on seeing _design thinking_ as a human-centered activity. A successful outcome exist at the intersection of desirable from users, technically feasible and viable for the business [p.11]. His works however ignore how social scientists usually also question their own theoretical, political or other perspectives they themselves bring into the design process [p.12]. 
Roger Martin focuses on what method successful managers used and how _design thinking_ gives businesses competitive advantage [p.12]. He sees _design _thinking_ as combining abductive, inductive and deductive reasoning. [p.12]. 

Given the diversity there is no clear description of _design thinking_. [p.13]. 
Different ways of describing it are: [p.14] 
- **Design thinking as a cognitive style**: Focus on problem solving. Key concepts: Design ability as a form of intelligence, abductive thinking. (Schön, Cross, Dorst)
- **Design thinking as a general theory of design**: Design as a field or discipline. Focus on taming wicked problems. (Buchanan)
- **Design thinking as an organizational resource**: Focuses on businesses in need of innovation. Key concepts are: Visualization, prototyping, empathy, integrative thinking, abductive thinking. (Brown, Martin)

[[johansson-skoldbergDesignThinkingPresent2013a]]
Proposes to discuss design thinking as two distinct discourses: 
- _Designerly thinking_ is used to describe the research rooted in academia about the designers practice and theoretic discussions about it. 
- _Design thinking_ for discourse beyond the design practice that is mostly by people without academic background, particularly in management. [p.3]

_Designerly thinking_ is rooted in over 50 years of academic research, with first resources mentioned in 1969 and a purely academic aim. Until Buchanan in 1992 a step-by-step approach with two distinct phases of problem definition, followed by problem solution, was the accepted model. Buchanan build on the older _wicked problem approach_ and suggested "placements" to contextualize design situations, allowing problem definition and solution to happen simultaneously. Buchanan further identified the four distinct areas of design thinking as _symbolic and visual communications_, _material objects_, _activities and organizational services_, _complex systems or environments for living, working, playing and learning_. [p.5]. Lawson and Cross in 2005 and 2011 derived practical, observation-based patterns from real-world experience, with Lawson suggesting a model of the design process as a series of process-driven steps and Cross framing it as a recursive strategy [p.5]. Krippendorff in 2006 initiated a "semantic turn" by defining design as the creation of meaning rather than physical artifacts. [p.6]

_Design thinking_ as a management discourse appeared in the early 2000's. 
IDEO, using its practical experience and partnership with the Stanford University, re-branded itself as "an innovation company", introducing _design thinking_ into the academic innovation discourse as an alternative to strategic management models gaining traction in 2009 [p.7]. Design thinking grew in popularity as an innovation tool by making design methodologies accessible to non-designers and managers rather than limiting them to creative professionals [p.8]. This concept evolved through three different origins: IDEO’s published set of methodologies, work culture and infrastructure - labelled as _design thinking_ by the CEO Tim Brown [p.8]. Roger Martins description of _design thinking_ as a cycle of generating ideas (abduction), predicting consequences (deduction), and testing and generalizing (induction) [p.8].

[Design Thinking Comes of Age - Jon Kolko - 2015]
_design thinking_ shift is about applying the principles of design to the way people work [p.4]. Businesses work with multiple complexities and need to make sense of them [p.4]. Best tool is _design thinking_, a set of principles - empathy, prototyping and tolerance for failure - among them [p.4]. 

A design-centric culture is described as
- **Focus on user experiences** Observing behavior to learn what people want and need, to build empathy with users [p.4]. 
- **Create models to examine complex problems**: Physical models, known as _design artifacts_, are used to explore, define and communicate [p.4]. They allow for nonlinear thought when tackling problems [p.5.]. For example _customer journey map_ was used to understand veterans interactions with the VA [p.5]. 
- **Use prototypes to explore potential solutions**: _journey maps_ explore **problem space**, prototypes explore **solution space**. [p.5]. Design companies use them as a way to communicate ideas and tend to iterate quickly [p.5]. 
- **Tolerate failure**: Things are not created right the first time, teams close on the solution by iteratively experimenting and pivoting [p.5]
- **Exhibit thoughtful restraint**: Solutions taking user needs in mind are also simpler than other [p.5]

Challenges employing a design-centric process are:
- **Accepting more ambiguity**: It is impossible to be certain how much value a better experience or mor innovation will bring [p.7]
- **Embracing risk**: Transformation is risky and requires leaps of faith. There is no way to guarantee the outcome of innovative attempts [p.7]
- **Resetting expectations**: _design_ is not the right tool for everything concerned operating a business [p.7]

[[micheliDoingDesignThinking2019]]
Notes that there is no generally accepted definition for _design thinking_ [p.2]. Among the literature some see it as organizational attribute, some focus on the traits at an individual level, some concentrate on tooling and some see it as (design) culture [p.2]. The term has varied origins, in academic writing many write about _designerly thinking_ while _design thinking_ established itself recently in management and is described as "an approach to innovation and creative problem-solving founded in designers' processes and practices" [p.2]. **Performed a literature review.**

Three definitions of _design thinking_ have been cited the most in literature [p.8]: 
- (1) Brown 2008: design thinking is "a discipline that uses the designers' sensibility and methods to match people's needs with what is technologically feasible and what a viable business strategy can convert into customer value and market opportunity"
- (2) Lockwood 2010: design thinking is "a human-centered innovation process that emphasizes observation, collaboration, fast learning, visualization of ideas, rapid concept prototyping, and concurrent business analysis"
- (3) Martin 2009: defines design thinking as "the productive mix of analytical thinking and intuitive thinking"

Applied models most frequently cited are: [p.8]
- **IDEO**: Inspiration, ideation, implementation
- **Stanford Design School**: Empathy, define, ideate, prototype, test
- **IBM**: Understand, explore, prototype, evaluate
The models share a common core structure and follow three main phases: [p.8]
- **Problem exploration**: Initial research to understand the problem to solve
- **Ideation**: Generate creative solutions and alternative ideas
- **Implementation and testing**: Build prototypes and continuously refine them through iteration

Through a systematic literature review and following card sorting exercise 10 attributes of _design thinking_ and 8 _tools_ have been identified [p.7] 
- **Creativity and Innovation**: Creativity and innovation is a central theme in _design thinking_ literature and practice, acting as both core drivers and outcomes achieved through methods like prototyping, trial-and-error, and abductive logic. 
- **User centeredness and involvement**: User centeredness is a core value of _design thinking_. Some frameworks advocate for direct involvement, referred to as participatory-, or co-design. Others describe empathy as "core value of human-centeredness". 
- **Problem solving**: _Design thinking_ is considered as an alternative to analytical, linear ways of solving problems, especially for addressing "wicked problems", where certain aspects can be improved but the problem not fully solved in a "right" way.
- **Iteration and experimentation**: _Design thinking_ utilizes an iterative, trial-and-error approach to solve problems. Tangible prototypes, mocks and sketches are used to experimentally explore ideas and get feedback from stakeholders. 
- **Interdisciplinary collaboration**: _Design thinking_ relies on bringing together people from different backgrounds into a cross-functional, multidisciplinary team to make sure the various perspectives are represented. 
- **Ability to visualize**: Visualizing concepts and ideas is central in _design thinking_ and can make abstract concepts feel real and vivid. This may be through physical representations, prototypes or sketches. 
- **Gestalt view**: _Design thinking_ incorporates an integrative approach that considers the context and systems in which a problem exists. It enables _design thinkers_ gain deeper understanding of a problems context and to challenge the problem statement, incorporating insights already gained into the reformulation of it.
- **Abductive reasoning**: Is assertion-based that imagines what might be rather than analyzing based on evidence of what is. Generates new knowledge by reframing existing assumptions to find best possible explanation or solution. 
- **Tolerance of ambiguity and failure**: Design thinkers must embrace ambiguity and failures as opportunity to learn early through iteration and low-cost trial-and-error cycles. [p.13]
- **Blending rationality and intuition**: Rather than replacing analytical logic, _design thinking_ dynamically blends rational, evaluative research with intuitive insights to drive innovation. 

Essential design thinking tools and methods found: [p.13]
- **Ethnographic methods**:  
	- _user centeredness and involvement_ Means to foster empathy 
	- _blend analysis and intuition_: Observational data complements quantitative data.
	- _gestalt view_: Understand problem in its context. 
	- _abductive reasoning_: Rich data can trigger "what if?" questions
- **Personas**:
	- _user centeredness and involvement_: Means to foster empathy
	- _interdisciplinary collaboration_: Serve as boundary-spanning object
	- _visualize_ key stakeholders
	- _gestalt view_: of the problem by ensuring users are represented in the process
- **Journey map**:
	- _user centeredness and involvement_: Foster empathy
	- _interdisciplinary collaboration_: Serve as a boundary-spanning object
	- _visualize_: Key stakeholders' experiences
	- _Iteration and experimentation_: by allowing to consider and test multiple user journeys
	- _gestalt view_: by encouraging contextual and temporal understanding of user experience
- **Brainstorming**: 
	- _abductive reasoning_: Provides a forum for that
	- _interdisciplinary collaboration_: Typically used during that
	- _blending of analysis and experimentation_: Reinforces through withholding judgement of individual ideas
- **Mind map**:
	- _interdisciplinary collaboration_: Serves as boundary-spanning object
	- _acceptance of ambiguity and failure_: by facilitating a process of sensemaking
	- _ability to visualize_ and structure complex systems and environments
	- _gestalt view_: by mapping multiple stakeholders' conceptualizations
- **Visualization**: 
	- _interdisciplinary collaboration_: Serves as boundary-spanning object
	- _ability to visualize_: underscores the importance to visualize solutions during ideation
	- _iteration and experimentation_: encouraged by informal nature of sketches
	- _user centeredness_, _gestalt view_, _blending of analysis and intuition_
- **Prototyping**:
	- _iteration and experimentation_ through provided physical means
	- _failure_ encouraged as early draft form
	- _interdisciplinary collaboration_ serves as boundary-spanning object
	- _ability to visualize_ underscores the importance to visualize
- **Experiments**: 
	- enable _iteration and experimentation_
	- _gestalt view_ through deep appreciation of the problem context
	- _failure_ encouraged for early draft form

Cluster analysis based on the 10 attributes. creativity and innvoation was present in all literature. user centeredness, problem solving, iteration and experimentation was present in 60% of artices. Those attributes have thus been excluded. Of the remaining 6 attributes, the following five clusters have been formed. These can be seen **to represent five perspectives on design thinking literature** [p.15]
- **Emphasis on interdisciplinary collaboration**: Cluster 1 highlights design thinking as an interdisciplinary team sport that breaks down organizational silos by training all employees to use design methods. Advocates argue that this scalable problem-solving methodology can be applied across any industry or context. However, critics note that widespread claims of its positive organizational impact remain largely speculative and lack empirical evidence. [p.16-17]
- **Emphasis on reclaiming design thinking as designers domain**: Cluster 2 advocates for reclaiming design thinking as a professionally trained skillset, arguing that core attributes like visualization cannot be separated from actual design practice. Authors in this group challenge the management-led idea that design thinking can easily be uncoupled from "design doing" or successfully adopted by non-designers without formal training. [p.17]
- **Emphasis on resilience in problem solving**: Cluster 3 highlights how design thinking serves as a cultural shift toward interdisciplinary collaboration and an increased tolerance for failure in complex environments, though debates continue regarding whether success requires professional design experience or simply a diverse mix of thinking styles. [p.18]
- **Emphasis on seeing and reflecting upon the whole**: Cluster 4 focuses on individual cognitive processes, emphasizing how professional design education leverages visualization and a holistic, gestalt view to navigate iterative cycles of action and reflection. [p.18]
- **Emphasis on learning to think like a designer**: Cluster 5 emphasizes a holistic, gestalt perspective that blends analysis and intuition, advocating that this mindset should be taught in business schools despite skepticism about whether non-designers can truly master the craft.[p.18]

[[[DesignThinkingQuick2020]] - Internet Archive - 2020 Version - interaction design foundation]
Describes the fundamental principles of _design thinking_ as: _Empathy_, _Reframing the problem_ to gain a more holistic look, _collaborative teamwork_ in a _multi-disciplinary_ team, employ _divergent thinking_ to explore possibilities and then later _convergent thinking_ to isolate potential solutions, rapidly modelling potential solutions and an iterative approach. 

Core ingredients of _design thinking_ are _reframing_, _empathy_, _ideation_, _prototyping_ and _testing_ - of which one or more are present in all described varieties of _design thinking_ processes. 

**Simons 1969** seminal work **The Sciences of the Artificial** seems to describe the design process as: _define_, _research_, _ideate_, _prototype_, _choose_, _implement_ and _learn_. 

**IDEOs** first expression of this process was the "Deep-Dive": _Understand_, _Observe_, _Visualize_, _Evaluate_, _Implement_. IDEO now uses: _Inspire_, _Ideate_, _Implement_. 

**Stanford Design School** (d.school), now known as Hasso Plattner Institute of Design teached _design thinking_ with the steps: _Understand_, _Improve_, _Apply_. They then moved to formulate their process as: _Empathise_, _Define_, _Ideate_, _Prototype_, _Test_. 

**Design Council of the UK** uses a process: _Discover_, _Define_, _Develop_, _Deliver_. They defined and make use of the **Double Diamond diagram** that indicates 2 cycles of _divergent_ and _convergent_ thinking.

[[[kellyDesignThinkingComputational2021]] - Design thinking and computional thinking - Kelly - 2021]

Term _design thinking_ has its foundation in research on how designers know, act and think [p.1]. The term has become ambiguous, former term is often also described as _designerly ways of knowing, thinking and acting_ and as _designerly thinking_ [p.2]. 

It is know also used as shorthand for the set of tools, skills and mindsets that are suited for teaching non-designers how to approach problems in a _designerly_ way [p.3]. 

Terms _design problems_ and _design thinking_ are related. Design is defined as "goal-oriented, constrained, decision-making, exploration and learning activity which operates within a context which depends on the designer's perception of the context" [p.3]. 

The term of **frame** appears in _design_. Within the frame design activity is undertaken. A frame might be referred to as a _situation_. [p.3]. Different definitions of _frame_ can be linked to Simon 1969. Knowledge of a designer is not stored in "discrete abstract chunks waiting to be deployed" but instead, when faced with a design problem, a frame is constructed as "complex assemblage of interrelated knowledge" [p.4]. 

Problems well suited for design thinking are those which frames with useful solutions need to be evolved in some way for a solution to become apparent. _Wicked problems_ are an example of this [p.4]. 

In design schools students are taught how to think about the user and context about the design. This guides denote strategies that implicitly change the frame in a direction that is useful for moving towards a solution [p.5]. Many activities in _design thinking_ are oriented at "expanding the frame of understanding surrounding the problem" [p.8]. Design patterns might be used by the designer to do so [p.11]. 

"A notable feature of design solutions is also that they are specific to the problem that they were created for" [p.5].
