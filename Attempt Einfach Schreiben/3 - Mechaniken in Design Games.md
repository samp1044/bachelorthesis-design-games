[Facilitating collaboration through design games - 2004 - Brandt and Messeter]
Constructing **scenarios**. Being both concrete and flexible [p.1] Deliberately incomplete to be open for negotiation and change [p.1]. Important part of the development process is that the designers communicate and negotiate with each other [p.2]. Similar to playing games in that **players have roles** [p.2]. **Rules** set the boundaries for whats possible and also structure the play of the game [p.2]. **Facilitator** taking an active role, driving the process forward by making suggestiongs and encouraging players to make moves [p.4]. **Game material** like game-boards, cards [p.5]. **Game materials that abstract real-life objects**, but are not too concrete to be open to interpretation - e.g. Game boards with concentric circles when designing an office layout, where players argue that it centers around the invidiual worker in their space vs. the board with multiple circles which can be interpreted as groups working together [p.6]. They function as **boundary objects** between people having various competencies and allows players to find out themselves what they find important but also how others interpret the material [p.6]. Being **tangible** helps keeping track of their functions and role within the negotiation [p.8]. **Acting** out **scenarios** from a users view helps to create empathy for the users and situations of use [p.8]. 

Boundary objects carry shared meaning [p.9]. Props make arguments tangible in the dialogue of design [p.9]. Having objects at hand to play with is important as it speeds up the process and help participants to focus [p.9]. They create a common ground that everybody can relate to and act as things-to-think with [p.9]. They allow different participants to read and interpret the material differently. They are rich enough in content to span the gap between different understandings. [p.9]. 

Shift of focus from the goals of the design process to the rules of a game arguably makes it easier to generate design moves. Studies showed that restrictions on idea generations can improve the outcome [p.10]  -> Mechanik: Restrictions of moves?

Playing by rules plays down external factors like power relations between participants [p.10]


[Designing exploratory design games: a framework for participation in Participatory Design? - 2006 - Brandt]
Organising participation is one of the cornerstones of designing [p.2]. Designing includes designing the artefacts and a set of rules for its use [p.2]. Designers have to understand the the language games of the use activity, users have to understand the language game of design [p.2]. 

Players seldom **compete to win** a game [p.3]. Concept Design Games create configurations using **game pieces** on a **game board** [p.3]. Pieces and boards can be **abstracted** to eliminate functional knowledge and former experiences players bring with them [p.3]. Concept Design games are primarily about **dominance** or **territory** [p.3]. Playing the game allows to learn about concepts the players hold [p.3].

**Change and Surprise** from the surrealist movement [p.3]. Some creativity techniques are based on **combining elements which normally do not fit together** [p.4]. Games inspired by surrealism use elements of change. They are based on a wish to get out of habits, experience new and be taken by surprise. [p.4]. e.g. a **dice** to decide by change which topic / question to work on next + using question to spark further discussion/dialogue (_Nordvest_ game) [p.4] **cards (game pieces) and game boards**, e.g. in Workflow oriented design games [p.4]. **constructing scenarios** to restructure the current situation and provide new insights [p.4]. Scenarios can be **enacted** using simple **props**. [p.5] **magic if** - when acting out a scenario the actress has to ask herself questions like "if the character was in this situation - how would she react?" [p.4]. 

**_User Game_**: **Tangible game pieces** Moment cards and Sign-cards. Sign-cards to provide a conceptual framework for stories created by participants. **Rules** how the stories are build using the Sign-cards. **Turn-taking** on how builds a story [p.5-6] to allow participation on more equal terms (also in connection with rules on game moves) [p.7]. **Directly involving designers and users in the Game** instead of handing over results [p.6]. **Open-ended fragments** from field data to allow room for interpretation [p.6]. 

**Game state and material placed centrally** so every participant can see the current state of information **_(Future workshops)_** [p.7]. **Abstracted game pieces** where participants can choose what they represent (open to interpretation, **boundary objects**) (_Workplace making games_) [p.7]. Abstract **game boards** that are interpreted by the players (_Landscape Game_ [p.8]) 

Each game has **explicit rules** for participation. Assignments are **open ended** giving participants the possibility to interpret and influence on what to focus on in the design [p.8]. Playing in an **informal atmosphere** [p.8] which is the most productive in creative work [p.10]. Videos and images from field work as more powerful **game pieces** [p.8]. Using **tangible pieces** participants use visual, auditory and tactile senses, which evoke more reflections and comments than otherwise [p.8]. 

The use of **stylized game-pieces** prompts participants to be explicit about their understandings and views when playing [p.8]. **Game pieces** function as **boundary objects** - shared objects that allow different interpretations at the same time [p.8]. **Game materials** and **rules** help to **bridge the gap between different languages of the participants**. [p.8] **Common Ground** that everyone can relate to [p.9]. 

**Scenario construction** can be used for creating and testing design ideas [p.9]. Generic props may be provided, or the users encouraged to produce their own in e.g. foam [p.8]. Having users create and explore scenarios in their own environment puts them on more equal footing with the design team [p.9]. 

[Formatting design dialogues – games and participation - 2008 - Brandt]
Essence of play lies in the intensity and the absorption of the players. Referred to as the **Fun element** [p.4]. Absorption has to be based on voluntary participation and an acceptance of the game rules [p.4]. 

_User Game_: Moment cards and Sign cards with videos [p.6] provide an **ambiguity**, partly by the open-ended nature of the video material. It matters what framing the Sign cards provide for the outcome of the game [p.8]. 

**Board games** are at the same time a format, means for collaboration and a vehicle for producing artifacts as reifications of the process.
