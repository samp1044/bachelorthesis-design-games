    \section{Mechanics of Design Games}
    Throughout the literature review a number of reoccuring mechanics have been identified. 
    The mechanics are subsequently clustered in 4 Clusters of effects they have on co-design sessions,
    when design games are used. Many mechanics can be attributed to multiple effects and are 
    therefore included in more than one cluster. 

        \subsection{Break existing habits}
        Breaking existing habits is a shift from everday logical thinking to generative, 
        imaginative and lateral thinking. It grounds abstract thinking into a concrete 
        reality so participants aren't overwhelmed by a blank canvas and overcomes potential 
        power relations participants are part of usually. 

            \subsubsection{Chance}
            Element of chance stems from the surrealist movement. "Games are often based on a wish 
            to get out of habits, experience new and be taken by surprise" \cite{brandtDesigningExploratoryDesign}. 
            This might come in the form of a dice, used to decide which topic or question to work on 
            next. 

            \subsubsection{Combining elements that do not fit together}
            People can be triggered out of their normal thinking patterns by, sometimes unusual, 
            associations, suggestions or actions writen on a card \cite{royCardbasedDesignTools2019}

            \subsubsection{Facilitation}
            A facilitator can leave out elements to increase the focus more on a specific topic or influence 
            which topic or question is to be worked on next \cite{kjaersgaardDesignGamesFieldwork2021}

            \subsubsection{Element of fun \& Ludic drive}
            The essence of play lies in the intensity and absorption of players. It is referred to as the 
            "Fun element". This absorption has to be based on voluntary participation and acceptance of the 
            game rules \cite{brandtFormattingDesignDialogues2008}. 

            \subsubsection{Presupplied Input}
            How topics are framed by the supplied game material matters to the outcome of the game \cite{brandtFormattingDesignDialogues2008}. 

            \subsubsection{Involving stakeholders}
            Participants should come from different backgrounds and explore cooperation through playing. 
            Design Games establish out-of-the-ordinary communication that breaks existing routines and 
            communication habits, allowing new problem solving approaches to emerge \cite{fikarGroundedTheoryZur2023}

            \subsubsection{Magic circle}
            The magic circle is a temporary sphere of activity in which the laws of ordinary life do not count. 
            It invites participants to think beyond the ordinary and envision alternative solutions \cite{vaajakallioDesignGamesCodesign2014}. 
            \cite{fikarGroundedTheoryZur2023}

            \subsubsection{Game rules}
            When playing, participants impersonate new roles that are defined by rules. Rules define what goal players strive to achieve, 
            how success is defined and what actions are allowed. Thus, rules establish creative freedom by excluding 
            ordinary life, while simultaneously forming its boundaries \cite{fikarGroundedTheoryZur2023}. 

            A defined winning condition and rules to guide argumentation are found to make it easier for participants 
            to reach consensus, which is described as more troublesome in classic co-design workshops with strangers \cite{zhangAddressingCulturalInertias2024}. 

            \subsubsection{Tangible game materials}
            Through Concept Design Games it has been found that Game Pieces and Game Boards can be abstracted to 
            eliminate functional knowledge and former experiences participants bring with them \cite{brandtDesigningExploratoryDesign2006}
